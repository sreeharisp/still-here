02 / The educational needAI-assisted visual design

A quiet object is not
an empty story.

Mission timelines tell us when exploration happened. Objects help us ask what exploration leaves behind.

The learning opportunity

Connect memorable artifacts to evidence: how far a rover traveled, what a simple experiment demonstrated, and why some instruments still matter.

The important distinction

Retired rover, historic experiment, active scientific legacy: these are not interchangeable. Not everything left behind is discarded.

Designed as an approachable learning experience; educational impact has not been measured.2 / 7

04 / The MoonAI-assisted visual design

Three artifacts.
Three kinds of legacy.

Apollo 15 rover

27.8 km

Distance traveled, using NASA NSSDCA’s reported value. Wheels expanded the reach of human exploration.

NASA NSSDCA: lunar rovers

Hammer & feather

In the Moon’s near-vacuum, the two fell together: a memorable demonstration of falling without significant air resistance.

NASA: Apollo 15 demonstration

Apollo reflectors

Passive instruments reflect Earth-based laser pulses for lunar ranging. Their ongoing scientific use means they are not discarded equipment.

NASA: continuing lunar science

05 / MarsAI-assisted visual design

The signal ended.
The record remains.

Spirit

7.73 km

Last contact: March 22, 2010.

Its journey helped reveal evidence of past environments on Mars. A final communication is not the same as the date a mission was formally closed.

NASA JPL: Spirit mission record

Opportunity

45.16 km

Last contact: June 10, 2018.

Its long traverse connected many observations of Mars’s watery past. The rover stopped transmitting; the findings remain available to learn from.

NASA Science: Opportunity
Distances describe rover travel, not distance from Earth. Contact dates are labeled explicitly.5 / 7
