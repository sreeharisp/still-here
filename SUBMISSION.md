## 1. Open the project on your computer

1. Keep `index.html`, `demo.html`, and this guide together in the `Still-Here-Space-Apps` folder.
2. Double-click `index.html` to open the museum in your browser. No installation, account, or server is needed for the intended standalone offline experience.

5. For a presentation PDF, open `demo.html`, choose Print, select Save as PDF, use A4 landscape, and turn browser headers and footers off. The print stylesheet is designed for seven pages, one slide per page. Check the preview before saving.

### Ready-to-adapt summary

Still Here, by team Forgotten Cosmos, is an interactive educational museum about objects left on the Moon and Mars. It pairs clearly labeled imagined first-person stories with NASA-sourced facts about the Apollo 15 rover, the hammer-and-feather experiment, Spirit, Opportunity, and Apollo laser reflectors. The experience distinguishes retired hardware from passive instruments that continue to support science. It is designed as a standalone, offline-friendly website with a seven-slide companion presentation. Our goal is to make mission history approachable while keeping creative interpretation separate from evidence.

### Ready-to-adapt description

The project asks what remains after a mission's most visible moments are over. Visitors explore artifacts, encounter an imagined first-person perspective, and connect that story to a factual record and source links. The Moon stories connect rover mobility, falling without significant air resistance, and continuing lunar laser-ranging science. The Mars stories connect rover travel distances and last-contact dates to enduring scientific records.

The Apollo reflectors are presented as a counterexample to the idea that all equipment left behind is discarded: passive instruments can continue supporting measurements without transmitting on their own. The first-person voices are fictional interpretations, not astronaut quotations or historical testimony. NASA mission records provide the facts; this project does not collect original mission data. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript support a lightweight browser experience, with AI-assisted text, code, and design. Educational impact and usability have not been established through completed user testing. Official challenge-listed data qualification remains to be verified before judging.
